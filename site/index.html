Acoustic data link · measured over the air

Your laptop can talk
to your phone.
Out loud.

Cyrinx is a research modem that moves real data through the air as sound — from an ordinary laptop speaker to an ordinary phone microphone. No radio, no pairing, no network. Cyrinx 2.0 measured 65.875 kbps MacBook → Pixel 7a while preserving the accepted benchmark's 0.25 s inter-frame gaps, versus 36.571 kbps before (about 1.80×). A separately labeled zero-gap research class reached 69.652 kbps (1.9045×, not almost 3×).

§1 Measured results

The Cyrinx 2.0 rows use strict ordered byte-verified goodput: a block counts only if its CRC-32 passes and its bytes match the transmitted payload at the same scheduled position. Historical rows retain their cited byte-verification method. For the accepted Pixel run, 375/375 decoded CRC-valid records passed expected-set membership; the verifier retained neither record uniqueness nor strict stream position. The Cyrinx 2.0 values are scheduled goodput: their denominator runs from the first chirp through the final frame data, including preambles, pilots, FEC, CRCs, and declared gaps, but excluding the 16,000-sample stream-end pad. Including that pad makes the zero-gap mean 68.636 kbps. Historical rows retain their cited measurement methodology.

Two schedule classes, reported separately

The comparable flagship retains the accepted benchmark's 12,000-sample (0.25 s at 48 kHz) gap between frames. Its 65.875 kbps mean can therefore be compared directly with the earlier 36.571 kbps result. The 69.652 kbps mean removes that gap and belongs to a separate research class; it is evidence about continuous scheduling, not a like-for-like replacement for the accepted benchmark.

The comparable Cyrinx 2.0 campaign won all eight order-balanced pairs (exact sign test p = 1/256), but its 98.481% block recovery was below the conservative baseline's 99.967%, so it failed the predeclared strict reliability gate. The zero-gap campaign also won 8/8 pairs and reached 1.9045× the old 36.571 kbps result, but recovered only 90.666% of blocks and failed the same kind of gate. These are measured throughput advances with quantified resilience costs, not an almost-threefold robustness claim.

Scope matters: the new results are near-field measurements on one Pixel 7a, one MacBook, and one placement: Pixel face-up on 0.5-inch soft cloth above the left function-key area, bottom mic near the built-in left speaker, Mac output 50%, waveform peak 0.18, and A/C cycling. SPL was not instrumented. Android performed tokenized stereo capture; it did not decode the new profiles on-device. A frozen build of the portable C receiver decoded them on the host, including two-microphone MRC, automatic receiver selection, and payload-independent pilot-local reliability weighting. An integrated ultrasonic mode and a lower-rate, pleasant-audible mode remain future work.

§2 How it works

In plain terms

Sound is pressure waves; a speaker can shape them and a microphone can read them. Cyrinx splits the audible spectrum into hundreds of narrow slices (like lanes on a highway) and wiggles the phase and loudness of each lane a little bit, many times per second. Each wiggle carries a few bits. A sharp rising chirp at the start tells the receiver exactly when to start listening, and known reference tones let it learn how the room distorts each lane so it can undo the damage.

The signal path

  1. Chirp sync. A 2→16 kHz sweep; a matched filter finds it to within a sample or two.
  2. Channel estimation. Two known full-band symbols measure the room's response per subcarrier — including the echoes.
  3. OFDM + cyclic prefix. 2048-point FFT frames with a guard copy so echoes land harmlessly inside the prefix.
  4. Pilot tracking. A profile-specific comb of known subcarriers tracks per-symbol phase and timing drift (the two devices' sample clocks disagree by ~25 ppm — ignore that and the constellation spins).
  5. Soft decisions. Each QAM point becomes log-likelihood ratios weighted by pilot-local error as well as global channel SNR — a locally noisy lane whispers instead of lying.
  6. FEC + verification. A K=7 convolutional code with soft Viterbi decoding repairs the survivors; CRC-32 per 256-byte block accepts nothing on faith.

Why not just crank up the constellation?

Because a higher-order constellation trades noise margin for bits. An early 64-QAM profile recovered 0 of 339 blocks at EVM 0.173, but that was a failure of that profile and receiver — not proof that these transducers can never carry 64-QAM. The route-qualified Cyrinx 2.0 profile combined stronger r⅔ coding, pilot-local reliability weighting, and two-microphone reception to produce the measurements above. Its lower block recovery is also why 64-QAM is not yet the universal default. This 16-QAM illustration shows cluster overlap; EVM alone is not a decoder verdict.

§3 It degrades. It doesn't die.

Acoustic channels are moody: move the phone six inches and a clean cell can become a reverberant mess. The Cyrinx 2.0 throughput profiles above have not yet passed the strict reliability gate, so the conservative fallback ladder remains part of the system. It was measured across placements on the same bench. The coherent values below are post-sounding PHY payload rates; they exclude the preceding sounding/probe transaction and amortize that cost only over a longer session. Including emitted sounding/probe samples gives about 32.852 kbps clean and 8.95 kbps reverberant, before host/ADB wall time:

  • clean placement46.915 kbps
  • reverberant carried entirely by two-microphone combining11.366 kbps
  • nearly hopeless non-coherent multitone floor, Reed–Solomon-coded138 bps

A separate earlier one-frame MRC diagnostic measured 5.154 kbps: it recovered 8/11 CRC-valid blocks where either mic alone recovered 0/11. That artifact did not retain a byte oracle, so it is a CRC-valid PHY payload rate rather than strict ordered goodput, and it was not a rung exercised by the closed adaptive loop. The 11.366 kbps row above has the stronger byte-identical evidence.

The rescue that matters most: maximal-ratio combining across the receiving phone's two microphones. The mics sit at opposite ends of the device, so the room's dead spots differ between them; combining per subcarrier fills each mic's nulls with the other's signal. At the worst coherent placement measured, neither microphone could decode a single block alone — combined, the selected link phase of the closed adaptive loop carried a 11.366 kbps post-sounding PHY payload rate with all 75 of 75 blocks recovered by MRC, through the same portable C library that ships, pinned by cross-implementation test vectors.

And when even that fails, a non-coherent multitone floor mode — energy detection with symbols longer than the echo tail, Reed–Solomon-coded with two-microphone energy combining — keeps a measured 138 bps active-frame rate flowing (131.282 bps including its unconditional 0.1 s tail pad; twice the earlier repetition-coded floor) where phase-coherent modems get nothing at all. Slow is a mode. Silence is a failure.

§4 Standing on prior art

Data-over-sound is a well-trodden field, and Cyrinx borrows its techniques gladly — OFDM, pilot tracking, convolutional codes are textbook material.

minimodem
the classic audio FSK software modem — Bell-era protocols, kilobit rates.
ggwave
compact FSK data-over-sound for short messages; widely embedded.
Quiet
OFDM/GMSK modem library for sound, the closest open-source relative.
Chirp / LISNR
commercial acoustic data transfer for payments and pairing.
Google Nearby
used near-ultrasonic tokens for device discovery.

What Cyrinx adds is the measurement discipline: end-to-end byte-verified throughput on named consumer hardware, a catalog of the four physical-layer defects that actually limited the link, a published log of negative findings so dead ends stay dead, and a receiver architecture whose graceful-degradation claims were demonstrated placement by placement — then implemented in a canonical portable C receiver that reproduces the headline result from retained Pixel stereo captures. Swift binds that core; Python is the research oracle and measurement referee; the Android Kotlin bulk decoder remains a legacy control implementation rather than a second source of truth.
